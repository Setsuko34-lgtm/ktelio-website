● Where the Council Agrees

All five advisors converge, independently, on the same top-priority bug: the fake social proof toast is disqualifying, not cosmetic. The founder's outreach runs through Reddit/Facebook communities specifically primed to detect and shame fake urgency — a fabricated "Maria just booked" loop is a live risk to the one channel that matters.

Near-unanimous agreement on a second point: the form's missing backend is goal-blocking, not a stopgap. The week-one success condition is "one real conversation with a real tourist." A form that fakes a success message with nothing wired behind it can lose that exact conversation without anyone noticing.

Explicit in three advisors, implicit in the rest: the page is over-built relative to the question being asked. 23 routes, tabs, count-up stats, scroll-spy animations — impressive craft, wrong allocation of a solo founder's scarce hours in week one.

● Where the Council Clashes

The real split is the Expansionist versus everyone else. All five peer reviewers independently named it the weakest response, for the identical reason: it reframes real failures as "missed upside" and pivots to roadmap ideas (pre-seeded proof, a second funnel, dynamic pricing) when the founder has exactly one job this week. That's not a legitimate contrarian view worth weighing — it's answering a different question. Its footnote that ktelmap.com already validates category demand is fine, just not a priority.

The more substantive disagreement is about how many things are actually broken in the measurement instrument. The Contrarian's distinct catch — that the gold "€5" CTA vs. the ghost-button "free" CTA visually pre-loads the ratio — was rated the standout insight of the round by every reviewer. Nobody rebutted it; the other four advisors simply hadn't seen it.

● Blind Spots the Council Caught

The peer review round surfaced a second layer of problems no individual advisor named on the first pass:

  • The "paid" click isn't a willingness-to-pay signal. No payment is collected at the €5 button — it's a lead form, same as the free path. The ratio may only measure willingness-to-fill-a-form-with-a-scarier-label, not pricing demand.
  • Nobody specified how the ratio is measured. No UTM tags, click counters, or analytics were mentioned as existing. Without instrumentation, there's no ratio to read.
  • Sample size naivety. An 85/15 split off 10-30 total week-one clicks is closer to noise than signal — a specific ratio is premature precision.
  • 23 routes silently truncate the sample. A visitor whose destination isn't listed clicks neither button — attrition before measurement even starts.
  • Nobody confirmed the form was actually test-submitted end-to-end before prescribing a fix.

● The Recommendation

Ship it this week, but not as-is. Fix the two disqualifying issues everyone agrees on: kill the fake toast (replace with something honest — "Just launched, be our first booking" — or remove it), and wire the form to a real notification path (mailto fallback, Airtable, Google Form — anything that reaches an inbox in seconds). Don't spend more time on animations or expansion ideas; that thinking is right eventually and wrong for this week.

Then go one layer past what any single advisor said: stop treating the free/paid split as a pricing signal — no money changes hands at the €5 click, so at best it's a form-friction signal. Read it as "curiosity vs. intent to be contacted," not "would pay vs. wouldn't," and don't trust any specific ratio until there are more like 50-100 clicks, not 10-20.

● The One Thing to Do First

Before touching the toast or the form: add basic click tracking (a free analytics event or distinct UTM-tagged links) to the free and paid CTAs. Right now there's an elaborate instrument built to read a signal that currently has no way to be recorded.

Full Advisor Responses
The Contrarian — rated strongest by peer review▸

This site will produce garbage data, and the fake toast is the reason.

Start with the actual measurement instrument: the free-vs-paid ratio. That ratio is only meaningful if the two options are presented with genuinely equal weight, so a skewed click-through reflects real willingness-to-pay rather than UI persuasion. But the hero's primary CTA is gold and says "Book my route — €5"; the free option is a ghost button, visually secondary from the first pixel. Every route card repeats that asymmetry. If 90% still click free despite the page working hard to upsell them into paid, that's meaningful. But if the split comes back 60/40, the founder won't know whether that's real signal or just persuasive design nudging people toward the button that costs money. He's baked a thumb onto the scale of his own experiment and will misread the results either way.

Second, the fake social proof toast is worse than useless — it's actively corrosive to the one thing this test needs, which is trust from strangers meeting "KTEL" for the first time. Reddit and Greece-travel Facebook groups are full of people who reverse-image-search and screenshot-shame fake urgency tactics. One person notices "Maria just booked" is generated on a loop, screenshots it, posts it back to the same forum the founder is doing outreach in, and the founder's credibility — the entire asset for a solo bootstrapper — is gone before a single real conversation happens.

Third: submitting the form does nothing. No email fires, no webhook, nothing. If a real tourist fills it out during the exact 2-5 day window that matters, that's a dropped conversation, full stop, indistinguishable from data loss.

Fix priority: kill the fake toast today, make the form actually deliver (even a mailto: or Formspree is fatter than nothing), and rebalance the two buttons to equal visual weight before trusting the ratio.

The fake social proof toast poisons the exact metric being measured. "Maria just booked Athens → Delphi" is a fabricated anchor that nudges visitors toward the paid path. You cannot A/B test truth against a rigged scale. This isn't an ethics footnote — it's a methodology-destroying bug.

23 routes, tabs, count-up stats, scroll-spy — this is launch polish for a company validating whether it should exist. Every hour spent on ripple effects is an hour not spent writing the ten forum posts that are the actual experiment.

LLM Council · Round 2

What Else Should Change

Follow-up review after the round-1 fixes shipped (fake toast removed, mailto form, equal-weight buttons, click tracking, KTEL explainer) — focused on validation-fit and mobile friendliness.
Peer review: strongest response The First Principles Thinker — 5 of 5 reviewers picked this independently, for reframing the question from "is this honest" to "does this complete a transaction," and for noticing the debug panel's ratio is itself corrupted by the broken free link.
Peer review: biggest blind spot The Expansionist — 5 of 5 reviewers flagged its "live counter" idea as manufacturing fake momentum from near-zero traffic, undoing the honesty work from round 1.

● Where the Council Agrees

Four of five advisors independently walked the same funnel and hit the same wall: a tourist picks a route, taps "Book it yourself," and lands on a Google search, not a ticket — the free path doesn't actually exist yet for 18 of 23 routes. In parallel, anyone who reaches for the paid path fires a mailto: link that fails silently on any phone without a configured email app. Near-unanimous agreement on a third point: the nav disappears entirely below 860px, taking "How it works," "Why trust us," and "FAQ" with it — exactly the content built to earn trust, invisible on the device most tourists will actually use. All three are hours of work, not a redesign, and the council agrees they outrank everything else currently on the table.

● Where the Council Clashes

Disclosure vs. reduction: the Contrarian and First Principles Thinker want to ship only the 3-5 fully-mapped real routes rather than carry 23 routes on a search-engine placeholder. The Executor and Outsider are more permissive — just relabel the button honestly ("Search official site") and keep all 23. The council leans toward the smaller, fully-real surface, because a mislabeled button on most of the list still teaches visitors "free = search engine," which biases the very ratio the week is designed to measure.

The sharper clash is with the Expansionist's "live counter" idea (turning debug data into a public momentum signal like "3 people checked routes today"). Every reviewer flagged this as fabricating social proof from near-zero real traffic — precisely the dishonesty round 1 removed. It also buried the mobile-nav bug entirely. This idea should be discarded, not folded in.

● Blind Spots the Council Caught

  • No dry run planned. Nobody proposed actually testing the fixes end-to-end on a real phone before the week starts — sending a real message with no mail app configured, clicking every route link, confirming what happens.
  • Tracking validity unquestioned. Nobody asked whether the localStorage click counter survives a page refresh, private/incognito browsing, or a visitor switching devices — if it doesn't, the ratio the week depends on is corrupted before it starts.
  • No operational tail. Nobody addressed what happens after a message lands in an inbox or WhatsApp — is anyone monitoring it, and what's the actual promised turnaround (the FAQ still just says "shortly").
  • Channel sprawl risk. Stacking mailto + WhatsApp + plain-text email as parallel fallbacks could blur the one clear signal a validation week wants to read.

● The Recommendation

Fix in funnel order, not checklist order:

  1. Fix the mobile nav — a plain "Menu" text link jumping to an in-page anchor list is enough; no full hamburger rebuild needed this week.
  2. Fix or relabel the free path — hand-map the highest-traffic 3-5 routes to real KTEL sites; for the rest, either drop them from v1 or relabel the button honestly so no click lands somewhere the label didn't promise.
  3. Fix the mailto dead end — show the plain email address visibly next to the button, and add exactly one more fallback (WhatsApp click-to-chat), not three parallel channels.
  4. Before the week starts, dry-run every path with no mail client configured, click every shipped route link, and confirm the click counter survives a refresh and private browsing.
  5. Reject the live-counter idea outright — it reintroduces the fabricated social proof round 1 deliberately removed.

● The One Thing to Do First

Fix the free-path routes (or relabel the button honestly) and fix the mobile nav before anything else — both need to be done before the validation week starts. Everything else, including the mailto fallback, is secondary to making sure the two paths actually being compared — free vs. paid — both resolve to something real.

The First Principles Thinker — rated strongest by peer review▸

Walk the funnel as a real tourist on a phone: they land, understand KTEL, pick a route, hit "Book it yourself" — and get dumped into a Google search, not a ticket. That's the free path silently not existing. If most clicks go there, the debug panel will show a ratio measuring clicks-to-search-results, not clicks-to-outcomes.

Fix: hand-map the 3-5 highest-traffic routes to real KTEL sites before launch, not all 23 — coverage over completeness.

Second: mailto fails silently on exactly the devices most tourists browse on. Add a visible fallback: a tappable WhatsApp link or plain "email us at hello@ktelio.gr" text.

Third: nav links vanishing under 860px means "Why trust us" and "FAQ" — the content built to compensate for lack of trust — are invisible to most visitors.

Everything else (tracking, banner, form fields) is instrumentation around a funnel with two broken links. Fix the links first.

The Contrarian▸

That's a dark-pattern shape: the only reachable action is the CTA, while "How it works," "Why trust us," and "FAQ" are unreachable except by blind scrolling. Fix the hamburger before touching anything else.

Second: "Book it yourself" opening a Google search instead of the real KTEL site is a bait-and-switch the moment someone clicks it. Either label it honestly ("Search for official tickets") or don't ship routes you haven't mapped yet — 5 real ones beats 23 fake-feeling ones.

Third: mailto as sole backend combined with a free-text contact field is a silent-failure trap. Minimum fix: a visible "opens your email app — no email app? copy this address instead" fallback with a copyable mailto string.
